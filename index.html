Language in cultural context

History & Culture

🐎 The horse in Kazakh culture

The horse has long been important in the life of many Kazakh communities: mobility, herding, communication, sport and status. This helps explain the prominence of horse imagery in expressions such as at qulağynda oynau and at shaptyrym jer.

🐑 Pastoral life

Sheep, camels, cattle and horses were familiar parts of pastoral life. Their behaviour and characteristics became comparisons in everyday speech: qoidai juas describes gentleness, while tüimedeidi tüiedei etu describes exaggeration.

🐈 Animals in English idioms

English idioms draw on domestic animals, wildlife and stories. Cats and dogs often appear in expressions about secrets and conflict; horses in advice about patience; birds and fish in expressions about opportunity and belonging.

📚 Folklore and shared knowledge

Fables, proverbs and oral storytelling preserve animal imagery. A fox may suggest cleverness, a lion courage and a sheep gentleness. These are cultural patterns, not universal rules.

Important: Cultural context helps interpret idioms, but it does not prove the exact historical origin of a particular phrase. Treat origins as confirmed only when a reliable source documents them.
About the research

About the Project

A comparative study of animal-related idioms in English and Kazakh.

Research aim

To explore how animal-related idioms express human qualities, emotions and everyday situations in English and Kazakh.

Hypothesis

Although the two languages may use different animal images, many idioms express similar human experiences and cultural ideas.

Objectives

  • Collect 50 English and 50 Kazakh idioms.
  • Explain their meanings and usage.
  • Compare similarities and differences.
  • Identify full, partial and absent equivalents.
  • Explore cultural associations.

Methodology

  • Collection and classification.
  • Descriptive analysis.
  • Comparative analysis.
  • Interpretation of cultural imagery.
